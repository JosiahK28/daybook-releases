Daybook privacy policy

Last updated 28 October 2026.

Daybook is a habit tracker with a game in it, made by one person (Josiah) in Australia. It works offline and has no account. This page says what it keeps, what can leave your phone, and how to get rid of it.

Who can use it

Daybook is for people aged 13 and over. If you are under 18, ask a parent or guardian before you use it. It is a tracking aid, not medical advice. It cannot tell you what is safe for you, and its targets are only the numbers you choose. If you are changing how you eat or exercise a lot, talk to a doctor or dietitian.

What stays on your phone

Everything you log: sleep, food and energy, water, exercise, spending, subscriptions, habits, your targets, and your game progress. It is stored in the app on your phone. I never receive it. There is no Daybook account and no advertising or tracking code in the app.

What can leave your phone

Each of these is described in the app under Settings, Data and trust, "What leaves the phone".

Update check and noticesThe app asks GitHub for the newest version and reads a short public notes file. This is an ordinary web request, so the server can see your internet address, as any website can. Nothing about you or your logs is sent. You can turn it off in Settings.
Crash reports (off by default)If you allow it, after a crash the app sends the error trace, the app version and your phone model. No habits, logs or save data. The server can see your internet address when it receives the report.
Anonymous usage counts (off by default)If you allow it, once a day: app version, the date, how many times you opened the app, your hero class, deepest dungeon floor, and how many habits you ticked. No names, habit titles, money or health data, and no identifier for you or your phone. The server can see your internet address when it receives them.
FeedbackOnly when you tap Send. The text you type, and your phone model and app version if you leave that on, go to me privately. Do not put anything in it you would not want me to read.
Health Connect (off by default)If you allow it, Daybook reads sleep, steps, exercise and hydration from Health Connect on your phone. It stays on the phone and nothing is written back. You can remove the permissions in Health Connect at any time.
CameraOnly to read a barcode, when you tap scan. Reading happens on the phone. Product data comes from a bundled Australian extract of Open Food Facts and is never sent anywhere.
Exports and backupsOnly when you export, or if you set a weekly backup folder. They go where you choose. If that folder syncs to a cloud service, it leaves the phone that way, under that service's rules.

What I do with what I receive

I use crash traces to fix bugs, usage counts to see whether features are used, and feedback to answer you. I do not sell it, share it for advertising, or combine it with other data. Crash and count records are kept in a database I control and are deleted when they are no longer useful. Crash reports are deleted after 90 days, usage counts after 180 days, and feedback after 1 year. Your internet address is never stored with anything you send. To stop floods, the server keeps a scrambled code made from it, the date and a secret, and deletes it within about a day.

Deleting your data

  • Uninstall Daybook, or clear its storage in Android settings: this removes everything on the phone.
  • Before that, use Settings, Data and trust, Export backup if you want a copy. The file is yours.
  • Crash reports and counts carry no name or account, so I cannot look up yours. To ask me to delete feedback you sent, contact me at the address below.

Your rights and contact

You can ask what I hold about you and ask me to correct or delete it. Contact: siah_kop@protonmail.com. If you are not happy with my answer you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (oaic.gov.au).

Changes

If this changes, the date above changes and the app's "What leaves the phone" page changes with it.
