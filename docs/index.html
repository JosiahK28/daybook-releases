The real world

Six small things a day

Tap to log. That's the whole job. Daybook keeps it fast enough that you actually do it.

Sleep

Bed and wake

Log when you went to bed and woke up. On target means inside a window you set, not an exact minute.

Energy

Food, in Calories or kJ

Log meals and your own saved foods. A snack doesn't finish the quest; the target is a band.

Water

Cups, not maths

Quick buttons you set yourself, from the app, a tile or a widget.

Exercise

Minutes that count

A weekly target, split across the week. Optional Health Connect hints, read on the phone.

Spending

What's left today

One honest number: your safe-to-spend today. Categories you can rename or hide.

Subscriptions & habits

Renewals and your own habits

See what renews and when. Add habits with +1 / −1, skip a day, drag to reorder.

The loop

Your day lights the torch.
You play the dungeon.

Logging earns XP, torchlight and shards. Shards and gold buy looks, never power. Combat power comes from playing, so you can't cheat the game by lying to a tracker.

Log your day

Finish the day's quests for XP, and one hour of torchlight for each. Streaks and trophies pay shards.

Torch burns, hero fights

While the torch burns, your hero explores on its own and finds gold, even when you're away.

Descend by hand

Play real runs with a thumb stick. Pick relics, pick doors, beat bosses.

Forge and dress up

Three ways to play

Pick a class

Each one has its own skill tree, its own look and its own rhythm. The Warrior is yours from the start; the Mage and Necromancer unlock with scrap.

Warrior

Swings first, hurts last

Fast melee that never swings at thin air. Every hit that lands stacks Guard, up to 40% less damage taken. Stop hitting and it drains.

Mage

Burn it from across the room

Fireballs, Meteors with a growing shadow, and Blink that leaves violet dust. Fragile, loud and fun.

Necromancer

Never fights alone

Raise skeletons, bone mages and a golem. Reap with a scythe, throw bone spears that come back.

Quick as a tap

Log without opening it

  • Widgets. Today's water, energy, spending and sleep with quick-log buttons, a quiet readout, a single big figure, or a moon that fills as you finish quests.
  • Quick Settings tiles. Pull down the panel and log water, a habit or a purchase.
  • Reminders that behave. Ten-minute windows, no special alarm permission, and a +1 button right in the notification.
  • Habits your way. Yes/no or counted, minimum or maximum, skip a day, drag to reorder.

Yours alone

Your data stays in your pocket

No account. No analytics. No ads. Everything you log is stored on your phone and goes nowhere unless you export it.

The app goes online for one reason: it asks GitHub if a newer version exists, and you choose whether it may. Nothing about you or your logs is sent. Backups and exports are yours to keep.

Questions

Why does Android warn me?

Daybook isn't on the Play Store, so Android and Play Protect treat it as an app from outside. The file is signed with the same key for every release, and every update is checked against it before installing.

Is it really free?

Yes. No purchases, no ads, no subscription. It's a personal project that friends can use.

Can logging more make my hero stronger?

No. Logging earns XP, torchlight and shards, and those buy time, levels and looks. Combat power comes from playing runs, so you can't cheat a tracker for it.

What if I miss a day?

There's a rest week, and quests show "started" or "done" honestly. Streaks can be saved with a campfire. Nothing punishes you for a missed day.

What about iPhone?

Not yet. Daybook is Android only for now.
